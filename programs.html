The programs, in detail

Four programs of 6 to 12 weeks, from first safe batch to a tested product line.

Program 01 · Safely, from the first batch

Cold process soap

Sodium hydroxide causes serious chemical burns, so we start with goggles, gloves, ventilation and a workspace with no children or pets in it. Then formulation through a soap calculator every single time, oil choices, superfat, trace, and reading a failed batch from photographs.

  • 8 weeks
  • Beginners
  • 3 hours a week
  • PPE before anything

Trace, pouring and patience during cure.

Weeks 7–8 · Troubleshooting

Soy, paraffin, coconut blends and what each demands.

A log, several wicks, honest results.

Weeks 7–8 · Fixing faults

Tunneling, soot, wet spots and frosting.

Program 02 · Wick, wax, testing

Candles that burn right

Tunneling, sooting and drowned wicks are wick-and-vessel problems, and they are solved by testing rather than opinion. We work through wax types, wick sizing for each container, pour and cure temperatures, and a structured burn test log for every combination you intend to make.

  • 8 weeks
  • Beginners
  • 3 hours a week
  • Thermometer, scale

Start this program

Program 03 · It has to survive the process

Scent & design

Fragrance behaves differently in hot wax and in raw soap batter, and plenty of oils accelerate, discolor or disappear. We cover usage rates within supplier and IFRA guidance, which scents hold, colorants that don't bleed, and swirls and tops that photograph well.

  • 6 weeks
  • Some batches made
  • 3 hours a week
  • Scale and notes

Start this program

Weeks 1–2 · Fragrance behavior

Acceleration, discoloration and fade.

Weeks 3–4 · Loads and limits

Usage rates and staying within guidance.

Weeks 5–6 · Color and finish

Swirls, tops and consistency across batches.

Weeks 1–2 · Labels

What must appear on soap and candle labels.

Weeks 3–4 · Records and testing

Batch logs, burn tests and traceability.

Weeks 5–6 · Costing

Pricing that covers ingredients, time and losses.

Program 04 · Rules, costs, markets

Before you sell

Selling changes the obligations. Soap sold with cosmetic claims falls under cosmetic rules, candles need proper warning labels, and both need consistent testing and records. We walk through what applies and where to read it — and we're clear that we are coaches, not lawyers, and that insurance and legal advice come from professionals.

  • 6 weeks
  • Making consistently
  • 3 hours a week
  • Batch records
